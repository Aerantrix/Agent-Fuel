# Timeline

When things happened, week by week. Weeks are the project's weeks, counted
from Monday 21 September 2026 as week 1; the final package is due at the end
of week 10 and the symposium is in week 11. Three files share the history and
do not repeat each other: this one says *when*; `methodology_changes.md`
says *what changed in the study design and why*; `iteration_log.md` says
*what broke in the stand and how it was fixed*; `work_log.md` is the
engineering log as written at the time.

Weeks 1 to 3 (topic selection, literature, team formation) are not recorded
in the repository; they are to be added from the weekly milestone
submissions.

| 1–3 | *To be added from the weekly milestones: topic selection, the first reading of the literature, team formation.* | | Project brief received (21 September). |
| 4 | The project proposal. The research question in its final wording. Major technology: agentic AI with tool invocation; supporting technology: fault-tolerance patterns from distributed systems; second supporting technology: decision telemetry. Method: a comparative experiment through scenario-based simulation, not design science. Fifteen scenarios in three groups, seven configurations × three repeats = 315 runs. Two measures held in tension: overspend prevented against legitimate tasks interrupted. Rejected: fine-tuning the agent, a second model as judge, telemetry-fitted detectors. | First code of the controller (`agent_fuel.py`). | "Spend Resilience in LLM Agents", four versions in the week; references [1]–[7], then to [17], with provenance notes. |
| 5 | Three failures written down in advance as expected to defeat the first implementation (scenarios 2, 6, 14), so that a failure predicted in writing counts for more than one fixed quietly. Testing in three stages: one scenario with no control, the same with two mechanisms, then the full sweep. | The code package for the developer; DEVELOPER and HANDOVER. | First architecture diagram; the fifteen-scenario document. |
| 6 | The first turn. The first hosted sweep on Haiku 5.5 showed that the agent does not loop by itself: 103 of 105 runs ended within five steps. The question went to the author: push the agent to persist, or report "current models give up by themselves" as the finding. Answer: prompt pressure becomes the experimental variable, and the pilot runs under three persistence conditions. Model decisions: Haiku without a temperature setting (the API rejects one), with and without thinking; Haiku 4.5 not used. | Review of the brief, seven fixes, git initialised. Domains written into the multi-domain tasks. Harness defects found: identical metrics for every domain, latency invisible, the "impossible" period reachable. The 50,000-token budget cuts the honest forty-domain task for the first time; the threshold is left alone rather than tuned on its own test. Local console with Start and Stop; team README. Pilot begins. | Work log begun. |
| 7 | The strict prompt's text fixed. The pilot ends (2,765 runs). The design document: the account limit modelled as a day of N healthy tasks sharing one allowance; the triple covered / not covered / not checked defined; temperature 0 ruled out as the main setting for Qwen. | Pilot database. | Design document. |
| 8 | The design walked through scenario by scenario with the author. Fifteen scenarios become nine: two measured time rather than tokens, one duplicated another, one was not a tool failure, two repeated the forty-domain task. The flapping service is redefined as healthy work the controller must not touch. Three prompt levels, with the retry hints removed from the error texts so that the prompt is the only variable. Four Haiku variants (thinking × effort). Decision to collect the default prompts of ten open-source agent frameworks, so that the conditions rest on published texts and not only on our own. Open: compute the step cap and the account limit from the records rather than run them. | Stand v2: per-domain metrics from a hash, a cursor, fingerprints without the request, the breaker's warning shown to the agent. Sixty pilot runs carried over. | Task sheet for the real prompts (EN and RU). |
| 9 | The second turn. Every control is computed over the recorded uncontrolled runs, because the stub is deterministic and the controller can be replayed over the record; new measures: the minimum at step 3, the excess, the share of excess, coverage. The retry hint becomes a fourth condition of its own. The framework prompts become conditions: nine of ten run, AutoGPT left out by the author's decision. Author's decision: the 50,000-token budget stays and is reported as a weakness, with the "mechanisms only" row as the explanation. End of week: unknown-tool calls go through the controller; LangChain on Qwen repeated three times; a live check of the full controller planned, because replay cannot reproduce the warn rung. | STOP after a successful call fixed; the parser fixed (81 of 431 runs had been ended by the stand), the four Haiku variants rerun. Real prompts run through a text path and a native tool path. `batch.py`, `compare_live.py`. Pull requests #1–#3 merged. | Methodology changes document; conclusions in brief; first draft of the IEEE paper. |
| 10 | The live check: where the controller stops without a warning, live and replay agree exactly; the warning alone leads Haiku to abandon the healthy flapping-service task, a result replay could not show.
